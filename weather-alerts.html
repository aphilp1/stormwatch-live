What is CAMS?
Convection-Allowing Models (CAMs) run at 3 km grid spacing or finer — fine enough to explicitly simulate individual thunderstorm updrafts and downdrafts. Coarser models (12–25 km) must parameterize convection using statistical rules; CAMs actually resolve it.

NOAA's National Severe Storms Lab (NSSL) runs several CAMs daily and posts side-by-side comparisons so forecasters can assess agreement and skill against real observations.

WindNinja Hindcast Library
12 fire events · 164 RAWS station-obs · 2017–2025 · Western US
HRRR's 3 km grid resolves synoptic wind patterns but misses what happens at the terrain scale — the ridge amplification, canyon channeling, and gap flows that determine wind speed where fires actually burn.
WindNinja is a mass-consistent, terrain-following wind solver. Feed it an atmospheric boundary condition and a digital elevation model — it computes a physically consistent wind field at the resolution of the DEM (30 m here; can go finer).
HRRR BC  →  WindNinja  →  terrain wind field  →  vs. RAWS obs
The Two-Run Comparison
Run 1 — Hindcast
HRRR's actual atmospheric analysis from the fire event + 30m terrain. WindNinja computes the wind field. Result compared to what RAWS stations recorded.
Run 2 — Enhanced BC
Same WindNinja solver and terrain, but with an enhanced atmospheric boundary condition — closer to what was actually occurring aloft. Shows how much of the error comes from the boundary condition itself.
